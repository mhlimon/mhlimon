An effective illumination system must provide adequate lighting for the intended activities while maintaining reasonable electrical energy consumption.

In industrial facilities, lighting performance can deteriorate because of failed fixtures, ageing lamps, reduced luminous output, poor fixture distribution and inadequate maintenance. A systematic illumination audit identifies these problems through measured illumination, connected-load assessment and comparison with applicable lighting standards.

## 1. Understanding Room Index

Room Index (RI) is used to determine the number of measurement points required for an illumination survey.

$$
RI = \frac{L \times W}{H_m (L + W)}
$$

Where:

- \( L \) = room length  
- \( W \) = room width  
- \( H_m \) = mounting height above the working plane  

The Room Index helps establish an appropriate measurement grid for assessing average illumination.

## 2. Measuring Maintained Illumination

Illumination is measured in lux using a calibrated lux meter. Measurements should be taken at predetermined points on the relevant working plane.

The average measured illumination is:

$$
E_{avg} = \frac{\sum E_i}{n}
$$

Where:

- \( E_i \) = illumination at each measurement point  
- \( n \) = total number of measurement points  

The measured value should be compared against the applicable illuminance requirement for the intended activity.

## 3. Assessing Installed Lighting Load

Lighting power density is calculated as:

$$
LPD = \frac{P_{total}}{A}
$$

Where:

- \( P_{total} \) = total connected lighting power (W)  
- \( A \) = floor area (m²)  

This provides a useful measure of installed lighting load relative to the illuminated area.

## 4. Identifying Maintenance Deficiencies

A lighting audit should distinguish among:

- Completely failed fixtures  
- Operational but degraded fixtures  
- Damaged or incomplete installations  
- Inadequate lighting distribution  
- Insufficient installed lighting capacity  

Fixture count alone cannot establish illumination adequacy. Actual lux measurement is necessary.

## 5. Evaluating Energy Performance

Illumination compliance and energy efficiency should be evaluated separately.

Useful parameters include:

- Maintained illuminance (lux)  
- Lighting power density (W/m²)  
- Fixture luminous efficacy (lm/W)  
- Annual operating hours  
- Annual energy consumption (kWh)  

A lighting system should not be considered efficient simply because it consumes less power. It must still provide the illumination required for safe and effective work.

## 6. Practical Improvement Measures

1. Replace failed and deteriorated fixtures.  
2. Review fixture selection and distribution.  
3. Improve mounting arrangements where necessary.  
4. Maintain protective covers and enclosure integrity.  
5. Introduce periodic illumination surveys.  
6. Maintain fixture-wise maintenance records.  
7. Evaluate LED replacement on the basis of lifecycle cost and energy savings.  

## Conclusion

An illumination energy audit is not simply a process of counting lamps or measuring electrical consumption. It combines room geometry, illumination measurement, electrical load assessment and maintenance-condition evaluation.

The most effective approach is to:

1. Establish illumination compliance first.  
2. Identify the causes of any deficiencies.  
3. Implement corrective measures.  
4. Verify the results through repeat measurements.  

**Engineering takeaway:** Measure illumination, assess energy consumption and inspect fixture condition as three separate but interconnected aspects of lighting-system performance.
